Step 4: Height, temperature and moisture maps

Climate system

The final system does not classify terrain from height alone. It generates three aligned maps and reads all three values at the same coordinate.

Why three maps are used

Height

Controls elevation and separates water, coast, lowland and high terrain.

Temperature

Separates colder and warmer versions of terrain at similar heights.

Moisture

Separates dry and wet terrain, such as desert, plains and forest.

Combined result

Creates more variation without requiring a different mesh-generation algorithm.

Different seeds and scales

heightMap      → seed
 temperatureMap → seed + 2, scale × temperatureNoiseScaleMultiplier
 moistureMap    → seed + 4, scale × moistureNoiseScaleMultiplier

Separate seeds prevent the maps from repeating the same pattern. Larger climate scales produce broader regions, so climate normally changes more slowly than small terrain details. Independent offsets allow each climate pattern to be moved without changing the terrain shape.

Why three independent maps were chosen

Compared with height only

The same elevation can support different environments

Temperature and moisture allow plains, forest and desert to exist at similar heights instead of making elevation the only source of variation.

Compared with a hand-painted biome map

The result remains procedural and seed-based

Every chunk can calculate its own climate values from world coordinates, which supports endless generation without a pre-authored world texture.

Compared with a physical climate simulation

It gives direct artistic control at a much lower cost

Separate seeds, scales and offsets make the distribution easy to tune while keeping generation fast enough for runtime chunks.

Possible future refinement

Add selected relationships only when they improve the world

For example, altitude could reduce temperature or water proximity could influence moisture. The current independent maps remain a strong base because those rules can be layered on later.
